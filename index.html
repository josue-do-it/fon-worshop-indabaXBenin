African Benin & Linguistic History

Languages Have a History: From Danxomé Royal Tradition to Digital AI

The Fon language (Fongbe) is not merely a means of communication—it is the living memory of the Kingdom of Dahomey (Danxomé), carrying centuries of oral poetry, royal proverbs, philosophy, and ancestral wisdom. African languages possess deep historical roots, unique tonal structures, and expressive symbols that reflect West Africa’s rich cultural heritage.

Oral Tradition & Wisdom

Proverbs, storytelling, and songs hold precise linguistic tones that convey complex cultural metaphors.

Orthography & Diacritics

Fon orthography incorporates unique Latin-based symbols (ɖ, ŋ, ɛ, ɔ) and tonal accents essential for correct meaning.

The Modern AI Horizon

Ensuring Fon thrives in the age of LLMs requires local benchmarks created by African researchers for African context.

Context & Challenge

Bridging the Linguistic Divide in Modern AI Evaluation

Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4, LLaMA, and Qwen have transformed natural language processing globally. However, their comprehension of Fongbe (Fon)—a primary language spoken by over 4 million people in Benin—remains severely understudied and prone to high hallucination rates.

Because Fon is a tonal, low-resource language with unique orthographic diacritics and complex grammar, generic benchmarks fail to reflect real-world model competence. In this 50-minute practical session, participants will actively construct a diagnostic mini-benchmark targeting Fon translation, tokenization accuracy, and cultural prompt comprehension.

Why Fon Evaluation Matters

Building indigenous evaluation datasets ensures African AI systems serve local needs accurately without losing cultural context or linguistic integrity.
